HELOC: use your equity, keep your rate

If your first mortgage has a low rate, you may not want to refinance it. A home equity line of credit lets you borrow against your equity and leave that first loan alone.

  • Home improvements and repairs
  • Paying off higher-interest debt
  • Funding a down payment on another property

Available for California properties.

Learn about HELOCs

DSCR loans for rental investors

A DSCR loan qualifies you on the property’s rent, so there are no tax returns or W-2s to document. It works for purchases, refinances, and cash-out on rental properties.

  • Single-family rentals and multifamily up to 10 units
  • Properties held in an LLC
  • Available in most states
Learn about DSCR loans

Cash-out and rate-and-term refinance

Replace your current mortgage with a new loan, either to take cash from your equity or to change your rate, term, or loan type. It can make sense when the numbers and your goals line up, and I’ll show you both sides.

  • Home improvements and repairs
  • Consolidating higher-interest debt
  • Changing your rate or term, or removing mortgage insurance

Available for California properties.

Ask about refinancing

Non-QM: bank statement and P&L loans

If you’re self-employed, your tax returns may not show what you really earn. Non-QM loans let you qualify using your bank deposits or a profit and loss statement instead. Rates are usually higher than conventional loans, and I’ll walk you through the trade-offs.

  • 12 or 24 months of personal or business bank statements
  • Profit and loss statements
  • Business owners, freelancers, and 1099 earners

Available for California properties.

Ask about non-QM loans

Reverse mortgages

For homeowners age 62 and older, a reverse mortgage can turn home equity into cash flow while you stay in your home. I’ll explain how it works, including the costs and the alternatives.

  • Supplement retirement income
  • Pay off an existing mortgage
  • Create a line of credit for later

Available for California properties.
