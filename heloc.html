What is a HELOC?

A HELOC is a line of credit secured by your home. Think of it like a credit card backed by your equity, with a much larger limit. You draw what you need, when you need it, and you pay interest only on the amount you’ve used.

It usually sits in second position behind your existing mortgage. That’s the key: your first mortgage, and its rate, stay untouched.

Draw period

For the first several years, you can borrow, repay, and borrow again up to your limit. Many programs allow interest-only payments during this time.

Repayment period

After the draw period ends, you stop drawing and pay the balance down over the remaining term with principal and interest. Payments are usually higher than in the draw period, so plan for it.

Variable rate

Most HELOC rates follow an index like the Prime Rate, so your rate can move up or down. Some programs let you lock part of the balance at a fixed rate.

What San Diego and California homeowners use a HELOC for

Remodels and repairs

Kitchen, roof, solar, an ADU. Draw in stages as the contractor bills, instead of paying interest on the full amount from day one.

Paying off higher-interest debt

Credit cards and personal loans often cost far more than a HELOC. Consolidating can lower what you pay each month, as long as you have a plan to keep the cards paid down. Spreading debt over a longer term can mean paying more interest overall, so I’ll show you the full picture.

A down payment on another property

Use equity in your home to buy a rental or a second home, then pay the line back down when the time is right.

A safety net

Open the line and leave it alone. With many programs, an unused line costs little or nothing in interest until you draw on it.

How much could you borrow?

Lenders typically cap your total borrowing (your first mortgage plus the HELOC) at a percentage of your home’s value. That percentage is called the combined loan-to-value, or CLTV.

Your possible line is about: home value × CLTV − what you owe.

The maximum CLTV depends on the program, your credit, and the property. I’ll tell you what you actually qualify for.

HELOC questions, answered

What is a HELOC?

A home equity line of credit (HELOC) is a revolving line of credit secured by your home. You can draw funds as you need them during the draw period, and you pay interest only on what you’ve borrowed. It usually sits behind your existing first mortgage, so that loan stays in place.

How much can I borrow with a HELOC?

Lenders usually cap your total borrowing, meaning your first mortgage plus the HELOC, at a percentage of your home’s value called the combined loan-to-value (CLTV). Your available line is roughly your home’s value times that percentage, minus what you owe. The exact limit depends on the program, your credit, and the property.

Is a HELOC better than a cash-out refinance?

It depends on your first mortgage. If your current rate is low, a HELOC lets you keep it and borrow only against your equity. If your current rate is higher than today’s rates, or you want one fixed payment, a cash-out refinance may make more sense. I can run both side by side.

Is a HELOC rate fixed or variable?

Most HELOCs have a variable rate tied to an index such as the Prime Rate, so your rate and payment can change. Some programs let you lock part of your balance at a fixed rate. Terms vary by program.

Can I get a HELOC if I’m self-employed?

Often, yes. Self-employed borrowers can qualify for a HELOC, and the right documentation depends on how your income shows up on paper. I’ve spent 22 years structuring files that don’t fit the usual box.

What are the risks of a HELOC?

A HELOC is secured by your home, so if you can’t repay it, you could lose your home. Variable rates can rise, payments can increase when the draw period ends, and the lender may freeze or reduce the line in some situations. Borrow only what you have a plan to repay.

Can I use a HELOC on a rental or investment property?

Some programs allow HELOCs on investment properties, though guidelines are usually stricter than for a primary residence. Ask me about your specific property.
